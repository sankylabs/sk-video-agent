* THE CHALLENGE


Our world is evolving faster than ever. Technology, AI and global connectivity are reshaping the way we live and work. The next generation will face challenges we can’t yet imagine. Alongside new skills, they’ll need adaptability, creativity, and resilience to lead the way. At this year’s World Economic Forum, a Future of Jobs report found that 86% of employers expect AI and other information processing technologies like VR, AR and big data to reshape their organizations over the next five years, along with robots and autonomous systems at 58%, and new energy technologies at 41%. The future needs makers. As technology advances and schools struggle to keep up, Steamoji aims to fill a critical gap by teaching kids vital skills they’ll need in the future job market.


* The SOLUTION


Through hands-on STEM learning and the development of a Maker Mindset, we empower kids to not just adapt to the future, but to build it. While we recognize that kids are growing up in a digital world, we encourage them to shift from being passive consumers to active creators of technology. To do this, we equip them with real tools and real skills that are used by industry professionals. You won’t see any Minecraft or Roblox here — kids start out with Tinkercad, Scratch and VEX Robotics and quickly level up to microcontrollers and professional coding and design tools like Python, Unity and Blender. Our Journey to Master Maker curriculum is designed to inspire a maker mindset while teaching your child fundamental STEM skills in coding, robotics, engineering, 3D printing, digital arts and more. We’ll prepare them with the future-ready skills they’ll need for the jobs of tomorrow while helping them to develop confidence and critical thinking skills. And finally, our gamified approach means progress is celebrated as kids earn OJI coins as they level up through their journey, while you get updates in real-time through our Parent App.


* MISSION & promise


Steamoji’s mission is to train the next generation of master makers. A maker is a hands-on learner, builder and innovator who uses STEAM skills, creativity and curiosity to turn their ideas into reality. However, a master maker also has the mindset to embrace failure, adapt and be resilient as they encounter challenges and solve unexpected problems that come up along the way. Our promise to you is that regardless of how long you spend at Steamoji or whether or not your child becomes a Master Maker, we will help your child to develop a maker mindset that they’ll take with them into everything they do. We instill core values that we hope Steamoji apprentices will carry with them for the rest of their lives. Exploration is the spirit of embracing change — we believe in teaching kids how to overcome failure as they experiment and discover what works and what doesn’t. Collaboration is about working with others in an environment that’s both competitive and supportive. Problem solving is about looking at the bigger picture from all angles, and the maker mindset is all about resilience and adaptability, as nothing is usually successful on the first attempt. We teach kids to constantly iterate and improve their solutions and empower them with grit and tenacity, while encouraging them to seek feedback from others in order to gain greater perspective.


* What is a Maker mindset?


So what exactly is a maker mindset? This is central to everything we do at Steamoji, as master makers will combine hard STEM skills like coding, robotics, engineering, 3D printing and digital arts on the left with soft skills like curious observation, structural awareness, procedural understanding, causal analysis and creative expansion on the right. While “Build to Solve” is all about learning to build like a maker through hands-on STEM projects, “Solve to Grow” is about learning to solve like a maker by understanding problems and finding solutions. Both sides are equally important in the development of a maker mindset.


* build  to solve
Let’s break it down a little further. “Build to Solve” is made up of hands-on projects that reinforce learning and keep kids engaged, and this is where apprentices build future-proof STEM skill sets across a range of broad subject areas. They’ll learn core coding skills through programming languages, building everything from simple programs to advanced games with Scratch, Python and Unity. They’ll explore robotics, where they’ll learn how hardware and software work together by building and coding robots with sensors, motors and automation in VEX, micro:bit and Arduino. They’ll develop core engineering and design skills through the real-world application of simple machines, structures, gears and hydraulic systems. They’ll bring 3D-printed creations to life and learn about printability, spatial reasoning and design constraints with 3D modelling software like Tinkercad and Onshape. And they’ll discover their artistic expression through drawing, animation, design and stop motion while mastering digital tools for visual storytelling and creative communication.


* Solve to grow
“Solve to Grow” is about planning, ideating and solving in order to create a mindset that extends beyond the build. Apprentices will build confidence and critical thinking skills as they observe with curiosity by asking questions, noticing details and sparking a sense of wonder (“This is interesting!”). They’ll break things down, map the parts and understand how it’s built as they begin to understand the structure (“I see how that works!”). They’ll imagine new paths, look beyond the obvious and consider what could be as they explore opportunities (“What’s possible?”). They’ll tinker, test and troubleshoot, trying different approaches and finding fascination in the details as they analyze the cause and effect of what they’re doing (“Why did that happen?”). And finally, they’ll invent new solutions, improve on what already exists and share bold ideas as they create and reimagine (“What if? What else?”). You’ll see the clear benefits of this approach as they build confidence and creativity and feel empowered to be able to do anything they set their mind to. 


* Journey to master maker


All of this brings us to the curriculum — your child’s Journey to Master starts here. Our curriculum has four chapters, made up of missions that focus on building specific skills and with structured learning outcomes. Each chapter takes roughly six months to complete, coming twice a week, so becoming a master maker takes approximately two years in total. In chapter one, apprentices complete core missions that will spark their curiosity in areas like CAD design and 3D printing, coding and robotics, engineering and design principles and the digital arts. You’ll see them get excited about technology, try new things and discover their interest in STEM, and you’ll be amazed by their imagination! In chapter two, apprentices go deeper into new technologies and equipment like microcontrollers and electronic components, building resilience as they break down problems and work through challenges. At this stage, you’ll see them start to think beyond a step-by-step plan in the process of discovering that mistakes and setbacks are part of the journey. They might be uncomfortable, but a bit of discomfort is good and you’ll quickly see their resolve grow stronger. Chapter three is about finding their problem-solving style, using new skills and materials like laser cutters or game engines to take on their biggest builds yet. You’ll notice their independent thinking skills grow and they’ll become more strategic as they design real solutions. Then, in chapter four, they’ll expand their STEM toolkit, flexing their adaptability as they prepare for their big capstone project. The capstone is the final step to becoming a master maker, where they’ll identify a real-world problem and design and build their own solution to this problem using everything they’ve learned. They’ll showcase their skills and maker mindset as they work with confidence and creativity, defining their own approach while still receiving plenty of support. At each stage of the journey we’ll check in with you for a parent consultation to get your input and make any necessary adjustments to their program, so everyone’s always on the same page. If everything’s going well, we’ll use this as an opportunity to celebrate their progress before moving them onto the next chapter. Once they complete their capstone project and become a master maker, we’ll award them a Steamoji certificate of completion, and they’ll have built an online portfolio of projects they can use in high school or university applications. They then unlock a whole new level of missions that will continue to challenge them into their high school years, and can form teams with other master makers to participate in high-level competitions that will help to nurture great connections and references for future education and career opportunities. The Steamoji program is designed to set them up for life.

Objection handling


1. Schedule (“I don’t think we can commit to coming twice a week...”).


We know how busy kids’ schedules can be. That’s why we’re the only after school STEM program that offers flexible booking, so you can schedule your sessions to recur at the same time every week or pick and choose the days and times as you go. Many of our families like to book two 90-minute sessions per week to maximize their hours, but some prefer to come once on the weekend for two hours and do two projects back-to-back. It’s entirely up to you and what works for your schedule.


2. Price (“I want to make sure my child likes it before signing up…”)


The monthly option is a great way to try it out for a month first, and we’re sure your child will love it, but if you’re still not convinced I’d be happy to offer you a trial month at the annual membership rate, so if you upgrade to an annual membership at the end you won’t be paying any more for the first month. If you decide not to continue with us after the first month, you’ll just need to pay back the difference between the monthly and annual option.


3. Distance (”We live too far away…”)


We have a number of families that live far away who come for two hours on the weekends to do two projects back-to-back. That way you spend more time in the area and make the drive worthwhile. If you’re unable to get here twice a week and can’t do a 2-hour session we do also have the lite option which only includes 4 hours per month. We hope that this is a temporary solution and you’re able to upgrade to a full membership soon.
